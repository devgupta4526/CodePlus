# 7. Embedded Figure

---

## 1. What is an Embedded Figure?

An **Embedded Figure** problem involves a primary simple target shape (the question figure) and four complex geometric patterns (the answer options). Your objective is to identify which of the four complex patterns hides or fully encompasses the primary target shape.
- *Embedded* means 'hidden within' or 'firmly fixed inside'.

---

## 2. Key Rules and Constraints

1. **Strict No-Rotation Rule**: By default, in almost all competitive exams (SSC, Banking), **rotation of the target figure is NOT allowed**. You must find the figure embedded in the exact same orientation (angle and alignment) as provided in the question. Only if no options fit should you consider rotation.
2. **Proportionality Matters**: The relative size and proportions of the lines in the target figure must closely match the hidden version. If the target is a tall, narrow rectangle, a wide, flat rectangle in the option is incorrect.
3. **No Overlapping Breakdown**: The target figure must exist as a continuous, unbroken set of lines within the complex structure. You cannot jump across gaps to form the shape.

---

## 3. Search Strategies

1. **Anchor Point Tracking**: Identify a unique feature of the target figure—like a sharp acute angle, a 'Z' bend, an intersecting cross, or a curved arc. Scan the options specifically looking for this anchor point rather than the whole figure.
2. **The Erase Strategy**: Mentally 'erase' or ignore the extra clutter lines in the complex option figures. Try to isolate the core skeleton to see if it matches the target.
3. **Trace with the Cursor/Pen**: In computer-based tests, trace the shape visually using the mouse cursor over the options. This physical tracing helps bypass visual illusions caused by intersecting lines.
