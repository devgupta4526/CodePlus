# Phrasal Verbs

> [!NOTE]
> A **Phrasal Verb** is a combination of a verb and an adverb or a preposition (or both) that creates a new meaning completely different from the original verb.

## Must-Know Phrasal Verbs

### 1. Break
* **Break down:** To stop functioning (vehicle, machine). *"His car broke down on the highway."*
* **Break in:** To enter by force. *"The burglars broke in through the window."*
* **Break out:** To start suddenly (war, fire, disease). *"Fire broke out in the building."*

### 2. Bring
* **Bring up:** To raise a child. *"She brought up three children on her own."*
* **Bring about:** To cause to happen. *"The new policy brought about huge changes."*

### 3. Call
* **Call off:** To cancel. *"The meeting was called off due to rain."*
* **Call on:** To pay a short visit to someone. *"I will call on you tomorrow."*

### 4. Give
* **Give up:** To stop trying or surrender. *"Never give up on your dreams."*
* **Give in:** To surrender or yield. *"He finally gave in to their demands."*

### 5. Look
* **Look after:** To take care of. *"She is looking after her sick mother."*
* **Look for:** To search for something. *"I am looking for my keys."*
* **Look forward to:** To anticipate with pleasure. *"I look forward to meeting you."*

> [!WARNING]
> Do not translate phrasal verbs literally! "Look up" doesn't necessarily mean looking at the sky—it means searching for information in a book or dictionary.
